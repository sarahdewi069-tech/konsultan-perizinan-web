Why Using a Licensing Consultant Is More Efficient

General Guide

Every company can, in principle, handle its own certification and licensing internally. The question is not whether it can be done, but one of efficiency — whether internal company resources are better allocated to handling licensing bureaucracy, or to core business activities.

Time Considerations

Handling certification and licensing requires an understanding of the processes, documents, and institutions involved — which is often only learned when a company faces that need for the first time. This learning curve can slow the process compared to when it is handled by a party already familiar with the workflow.

Internal Resource Considerations

A company's internal legal or compliance team generally handles various other responsibilities beyond technical licensing, such as contracts, corporate compliance, or day-to-day operational legal issues. Adding the responsibility of handling detailed technical certification can divert focus from that team's main priorities.

Procedural Error Risk Considerations

Small errors in submission — whether in document completeness or non-compliance with the latest provisions — can cause the process to be repeated from the start. A party that regularly handles similar processes is generally more accustomed to anticipating such potential errors before submission.

When Handling It Internally Still Makes Sense

For companies with a large compliance team that already has experience repeatedly handling certain licensing categories, handling it internally can still be efficient. Using a consultant is usually more relevant when a company faces a new licensing category, has limited internal resources, or needs several types of licenses simultaneously within a short time frame.

What to Consider When Choosing a Consultant

  • Experience handling licensing categories relevant to your needs.
  • Process transparency — whether the stages and status of the work can be monitored by your internal team.
  • Clarity of scope of work and time estimates from the start, not promises of speed without clear basis.
The decision to use a consultant or handle it internally should be adjusted to each company's capacity and priorities, not applied uniformly to all conditions.

Summary

Using a licensing consultant is fundamentally a resource allocation decision — offloading the technical bureaucratic burden so the internal team can focus on core business activities, provided the consultant chosen can deliver a transparent and accountable process.
