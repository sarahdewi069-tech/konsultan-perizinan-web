Understanding NPB: Product Registration Number and Its Function

NPB

The Product Registration Number (NPB) is a form of compliance that is fairly often overlooked, as it is not as well-known as SNI or BPOM marketing authorization. However, for certain product categories, NPB remains a mandatory requirement before a product can legally circulate in the Indonesian market.

What Is NPB

NPB is a registration number issued to a product as proof that it has been registered in the circulating goods surveillance system, in accordance with the category and provisions governing it. This registration is part of the market surveillance mechanism to ensure that circulating products meet certain requirements.

Why NPB Is Often Overlooked

Unlike SNI or BPOM which are more widely known, the NPB requirement for certain product categories is sometimes not a primary focus for a company's compliance team — especially if the company is new to that product category, or the product is an import that previously did not require similar registration in its country of origin.

Consequences If NPB Is Not Fulfilled

  • The product risks being unable to be officially distributed through retail or formal distributor channels.
  • Imported products risk being held during the customs process if the category requires NPB as a supporting document.
  • The company risks facing market surveillance action if a product requiring NPB is found circulating without a valid registration number.

Initial Steps a Company Should Take

The most basic step is to confirm whether your product category is on the list requiring NPB. Because the scope of this category can change and is not always widely publicized, direct confirmation with a party that understands current provisions is a safer step than relying on assumptions from similar product categories.

Product categories requiring NPB may change following applicable circulating goods surveillance policy. The information in this article is general in nature.

Summary

NPB is a registration obligation that is easily overlooked due to its lower profile, yet its consequences remain significant for product distribution. Confirming this obligation status early helps a company avoid distribution obstacles down the line.
